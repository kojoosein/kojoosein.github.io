Modular Snap-Fit 3D-Printed 5-Inch Quadcopter Airframe

DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory, Manufacturing Science and Technology Branch Research Intern, DoW HBCU/MI Summer Research Internship Program Complete Jun 2026 – Aug 2026

I designed and fabricated a modular quadcopter airframe with snap-fit joints so structural components could be replaced without fasteners, tools, or adhesives.

drag to rotate · scroll to zoom

The problem

A printed aircraft is only cheap if a broken arm is a five-minute swap rather than a rebuild. I designed the airframe around snap-fit joints so the parts that break most often come off without tools, which is what makes an additively manufactured vehicle genuinely field-repairable rather than merely inexpensive to produce.

What I did

  • CAD modeled and 3D printed a fully printable modular 5-inch quadcopter airframe in Fusion 360 using snap-fit joints, so arms and structural components can be swapped in the field without fasteners, tools, or adhesives - the failure mode that normally grounds a printed aircraft after a crash.
  • Sized arm cross-sections against motor thrust and landing loads while accounting for printed-layer anisotropy, print orientation, motor fastener placement, and propeller clearance, keeping the airframe printable without support material.

Results

Snap-fit modular assembly: arms and structural components replaceable without fasteners or tools

Adaptive Fault-Tolerant Flight Control for Elevon Failure

Aerial Robotics and Experimental Autonomy Lab (AREAL), Georgia Institute of Technology SURE Research Intern May 2024 – Aug 2024

Detecting a failed elevon from the aircraft's own response, then reallocating control authority fast enough to keep flying.

Hero image drop the file in and rebuild — no code change needed

The problem

An aircraft with elevons uses the same surfaces for pitch and roll, so a jammed elevon couples both axes at once. At AREAL I supported the estimation and test planning side of that problem and owned the physical side, iterating a rudder redesign three times, because control theory ends at a servo horn with real geometry. That summer is what drew me back to the lab for my PhD.

What I did

  • Supported a Kalman filter based fault estimation effort for fixed wing elevon failure in MATLAB and Simulink, working through how comparing expected against observed aircraft response lets a degraded control surface be detected and its authority reallocated in flight.
  • Helped prepare the test plan for hardware in the loop simulation, defining the setup needed to expose the timing and interface behavior that pure software simulation cannot show.
  • Developed and iterated a rudder redesign through 3 CAD revisions, evaluating control surface area, moment arm, hinge placement, and servo linkage geometry to recover control authority lost to the failed elevon.

Results

~3 design iterations of a rudder redesign connecting control authority to physical actuation

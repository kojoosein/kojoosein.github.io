Streamlining Processes of sUAS for Rapid Deployment

DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory, Manufacturing Science and Technology Branch Research Intern, DoW HBCU/MI Summer Research Internship Program Complete Jun 2026 – Aug 2026

I redesigned a printable quadcopter frame to simplify assembly for non-specialist personnel and reduce frame weight from 600 g to 290 g.

Public-release material only.

DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory

The problem

A 3D-printable aircraft is only useful in the field if someone who did not design it can build and fly it under time pressure. My second summer at ARL treated that as the engineering problem: redesigning printable components of a 10-inch quadcopter so the airframe assembles with fewer specialized steps, integrating an FPV camera system, and writing the documentation that turns a research artifact into something deployable — then measuring whether repeated builds actually got faster. They did: I delivered the documentation to an Army infantry division of more than ten thousand soldiers, turning a full workday of scattered instructions into a 2-3 hour protocol. It connected my additive-manufacturing and airframe-design background to the operational question of who actually has to use the hardware.

What I did

  • Redesigned printable structural components of a fully 3D-printable sUAS quadcopter in Fusion 360, eliminating screws and fasteners to cut frame weight 52% (600 g to 290 g) while removing the assembly steps that required specialized knowledge, so non-specialist personnel could build the aircraft correctly under time pressure.
  • Authored assembly guides and configuration checklists validated through repeated build exercises measuring assembly time and system readiness; delivered to a U.S. Army infantry division of 10,000+ soldiers, cutting a build from a full workday of hunting scattered online instructions to a repeatable 2-3 hour protocol.
  • Designed and fabricated a mounting solution integrating the Avatar HD Kit V2 FPV camera while preserving field of view and airframe compatibility, extending the platform's reconnaissance capability without a redesign of the vehicle.
  • Presented the work at the 2026 DoW HBCU/MI SRIP Intern Symposium (public release, Distribution Statement A).

Results

Cut frame weight 52% (600 g to 290 g) by eliminating screws and fasteners from the printed airframe
Cut sUAS build time by more than half: from a full workday to 2-3 hours (soldering-speed dependent)
Assembly documentation delivered to a U.S. Army infantry division of 10,000+ soldiers
Public-release symposium poster and abstract (Distribution Statement A), 2026 DoW HBCU/MI SRIP

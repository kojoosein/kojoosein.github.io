Two-Axis 3D-Printed UAV Optical Gimbal

DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory Research Intern, DoD HBCU/MI Summer Research Internship Program Complete May 2025 – Aug 2025

I designed and prototyped a two-axis camera gimbal with approximately 90 degrees of travel per axis, positioning the camera mass near the rotation axes to reduce required servo torque.

Public-release material only.

The problem

At DEVCOM ARL I learned what defense-grade engineering rigor looks like: design reviews, documentation standards, and secure workflows around what is mechanically a simple object — a printed two-axis camera gimbal — whose every gram, hinge load, and wire path had to be defended. The experience connected my additive-manufacturing background to operational sensing hardware and shaped my interest in a DoD-adjacent research career.

What I did

  • Designed and prototyped a two-axis 3D-printed gimbal in Fusion 360 for an Arducam optical sensor, reaching roughly 90 degrees of useful travel per axis while aligning camera mass near each rotation axis to reduce required servo torque and payload mass on the aircraft.
  • Applied structural analysis and vibration-isolation principles to optical sensor mounting for fixed-wing flight, trading printed wall thickness against inertia and managing wire clearance across the full motion envelope so pointing would hold under flight loads.
  • Worked within defense documentation and design-review practice, producing a public-release poster documenting the prototype and its mechanical and flight validation plan.

My name is Kojo Osei-Ntansah. I am a mechanical engineer and a PhD student in Aerospace Engineering at Georgia Tech, advised by Dr. Jonathan Rogers in the Aerial Robotics and Experimental Autonomy Lab. I design, build, and test small uncrewed aircraft, working across aircraft structures, autonomy software, payload integration, and flight test.

Over the past three years, my work has included more than 50 flight tests across five quadrotor platforms, two first-author AIAA SciTech papers, and two summers at DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory. I am interested in how the whole aircraft works together and in developing autonomous aerial systems that can be used reliably in the field.

About

Background

I am based in Atlanta, where I am pursuing my PhD in Aerospace Engineering at Georgia Tech. My current research focuses on estimating the positions of radio-tagged wildlife using measurements collected from an uncrewed aircraft.

I earned my B.S. in Mechanical Engineering from Howard University, graduating summa cum laude as a Karsh STEM Scholar. My interests span aircraft design, autonomy, sensing, and manufacturing. I want to keep developing the skills to take an aircraft from an initial design through integration and flight testing.

Outside of engineering, I served as captain of Howard's men's lacrosse team and on the executive board of its American Society of Mechanical Engineers chapter. I am interested in continuing to grow as both an engineer and a team leader.
